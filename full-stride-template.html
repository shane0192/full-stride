This Week in Research

The 12-Minute Protocol That's Changing How Researchers Think About Aging Muscle

A new study out of the University of Copenhagen is making waves in geroscience circles — and for once, the headlines aren't overselling it. Researchers followed 312 adults aged 58–74 over 16 weeks, comparing four different resistance training protocols. One group trained for 12 focused minutes. Another for 45.

The result? Muscle protein synthesis rates were nearly identical across groups — but the short-protocol group showed significantly better adherence at 16 weeks. Which means on a real-world population level, the "worse" workout may actually be far more effective. The best exercise is the one you actually do.

There are caveats — there always are. The researchers specifically controlled for intensity, and the 12-minute sessions were not casual. But the implications for sustainable, lifelong training habits are real. We broke down the full methodology, the practical takeaways, and what it means for how you structure your week.

The Shelf Check — Is It Worth It?

Momentous Collagen Peptides

The science is real, but the dose and timing matter more than the brand.

Collagen peptides have become a staple in the 55+ supplement market, and for some genuinely good reasons. Type I collagen — the kind relevant to skin, tendons, and cartilage — does appear to have measurable effects when combined with vitamin C and taken around exercise. The research there is reasonably solid.

Momentous's formulation is clean — no artificial sweeteners, third-party tested, and they use a 15g dose which hits the evidence-based threshold. The joint support claims on the packaging are more aggressive than the data supports, but that's industry-standard overreach. The product itself is fine. Whether you need it depends on your protein intake and training load.

✓ Worth It

The Number

38%

Reduction in all-cause mortality risk associated with meeting the WHO's combined aerobic + strength training guidelines — according to a 2024 meta-analysis of 11 longitudinal studies covering 480,000+ adults. Neither type of exercise alone got close to that number.

 

The One Thing

If you do nothing else this week...

Do 10 minutes of resistance training. Doesn't matter what. Bodyweight squats count. The habit matters more than the exercise right now — start there.
