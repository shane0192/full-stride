Issue #003 · Wednesday: The Fix · ~3 min read

 

A few weeks ago I watched a 67-year-old man at my gym out-carry everyone in the room. Two 70-pound dumbbells, slow and steady, like he was taking a stroll. Nobody said anything but everyone noticed.

Turns out, that image is basically a clinical biomarker. This week's Fix is about grip strength — and the research behind it is one of those things that's so well-replicated, so consistent across populations, that I genuinely can't explain why it's not on every doctor's checklist. Spoiler: it predicts how long you'll live better than your blood pressure does.

Three exercises. Two sessions a week. One $20 tool if you want the actual number. That's it.

— The Full Stride Team

The Fix
Strength
Longevity

Your grip strength predicts your lifespan better than your blood pressure.

Researchers tracked 139,691 adults across 17 countries and found that every 11-pound (5kg) drop in grip strength is associated with a 16% higher risk of dying from any cause. Not correlated with other risk factors — better than them. Grip strength outperformed systolic blood pressure as a predictor of cardiovascular death, even after controlling for age, smoking, physical activity, and diet. (Leong et al., The Lancet, 2015.)

Your doctor probably checks your blood pressure every visit. When's the last time anyone squeezed your hand for science?

Here's the part that doesn't get enough attention: grip strength is also a brain story. A meta-analysis of 14 longitudinal cohort studies found low grip strength is associated with 54% higher dementia risk and nearly double the risk of cognitive decline. A 2022 JAMA Network Open study of 190,000+ adults found that poor midlife grip predicted measurable cognitive decline a full decade later. The hand is apparently writing checks the brain has to cash.

The Action

This week, add one of these — two sessions:

1. Farmer's Carries

Grab the heaviest dumbbells you can walk with. Walk 40–60 feet. Rest. Repeat 3–5 sets.

2. Dead Hangs

Hang from a pull-up bar. Work toward 60 seconds. 3 sets. Harder than it sounds.

3. Plate Pinches

Hold two weight plates smooth-side out, 30 seconds per hand. Correlates most closely with Jamar dynamometer scores — the same tool used in the mortality research.

Benchmark: Men 60–64 should be in the 38–42 kg range; women 60–64 around 22–26 kg. Red flags: under 27 kg for men, under 16 kg for women. A $20 hand dynamometer on Amazon will tell you where you stand.

The Number

16%

How much your all-cause mortality risk increases for every 11-pound drop in grip strength — across 139,691 people, 17 countries, fully adjusted for every major confounding variable. Not a niche finding. One of the most replicated results in longevity research you've probably never been told about.

The One Thing

If you do nothing else this week...

Pick up something heavy, carry it across the room, and do it again Thursday — your future self will have a firmer handshake and a sharper mind.
